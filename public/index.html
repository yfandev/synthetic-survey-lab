Three things run here. Drex answers the survey questions, live. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite is a top general-purpose AI we measure against: we asked it the same 11 pairs once each and saved the answers, so its scores stay fixed. Nace NDI reads the questionnaire PDF you upload — a survey, a ballot, a feedback form — and pulls out every question with its options and the page it came from, live. In the wording test Gemini runs live again to write alternative phrasings.

Why a team runs this before paying for a survey

Fieldwork costs thousands of dollars and takes weeks, and by then a bad question is already in the field. A simulated panel shows you what you would get first.

How this is measured, and all 11 checks

Every prediction is scored against the real survey answer. On accuracy Drex finishes behind the frontier model and behind a plain national average, and both are printed next to its own.

Four survey questions — spending on education (Q1), happiness (Q2), trust in people (Q4), and whether government should do more (Q5) — asked of three groups: 18–29 with a bachelor's degree, people 65 and over, and Democrats. That is 12 combinations; the trust question for 18–29 is dropped because only 14 respondents fall in that cell, which is too few to call a real answer.

Each call was timed end to end on the same machine, with Gemini on its free tier: a real measurement of both models side by side. The Gemini answers come from one pass, one question at a time, saved as they arrived.

Predict a group

Pick a question and a group. Drex answers with a share for every option, and the real survey answer appears beside it.

The real answers come from the General Social Survey, which has interviewed a few thousand Americans a year since 1972 and publishes what they said. The 2024 round covered 3,986 people by interview, web and phone, and we score against those answers.

Scoreboard

Every pair we checked, scored once. Error is the share of answers sitting on the wrong option: 0 is perfect, 0.27 means 27% misplaced. Panel run

Ask the same question as 100 different real respondents, one call each, with each person's recorded demographics as the only input, then add up the answers. It shows how fast Drex works through a panel.

Wording test

A prediction is useful when it survives a change of words. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite writes four other ways to ask the same question. Drex checks that each one means the same thing (score 0.90 or higher), then we recompute the answer for each wording. If the answer moves, the prediction is fragile.

Real answers: General Social Survey 2024, NORC at the University of Chicago · weighted so the sample matches the national population.
