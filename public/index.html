Drex vs. the giants, measured

The case that matters is a question nobody has asked yet: a campaign wants to know how a group would answer before paying for fieldwork, and no real answer exists to look up. To know whether to trust that prediction, we grade it on questions where the answer already exists — the national survey asked them, so we can measure exactly how far off Drex is.

The numbers below are already measured, so nothing here needs a click.

What runs here: Drex makes every prediction (live). Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite is the frontier model we compare against. We asked it the same 11 question-and-group pairs once each, in a fixed order, and saved the answers; it never runs on the scoreboard, and the only place it runs live is the wording test. Nace NDI, Nace's document parser, reads your PDF (live).

Predict a group

Pick a question, describe the group, and Drex answers with a share for every option. Where the General Social Survey (GSS) asked the same question, the real result appears beside it.

The GSS is a national survey run by NORC at the University of Chicago since 1972. Each round asks a few thousand Americans a long list of questions, and many of those questions come back every round. The 2024 round used in-person, web and phone interviews and has 3,986 respondents. We use its answers as the real result.

Scoreboard

Every question we could check, scored once. Lower is better: 0 means the answer matched the real survey exactly, 1 means it had nothing in common. The national average column gives every group the same answer, which is the thing to beat.

Panel run

One model, many people: Drex is asked the same question as N different real respondents, using only each person's recorded demographics, and the answers are added up. This measures throughput, not a race between models — no other model is called here.

Reword test

Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite writes four other ways to ask the question. Drex keeps only the ones it scores as the same question (0.90 or higher), then we recompute the answer for each.

Real answers: General Social Survey 2024, NORC at the University of Chicago · weighted so the sample matches the national population.
