Three things run here. Drex answers the survey questions, live. Group answers are blended with the whole-country split (90% country, 10% Drex) before scoring. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite answers the same questions in the same run, live, so the two are compared like for like; the numbers above come from 8 recorded pairs. Nace NDI reads every page of the questionnaire PDF you upload — a survey, a ballot, a feedback form — and returns each question with its options and the page it came from, live. A wording check closes each run: Gemini writes other phrasings and Drex decides which ones still ask the same thing.

Why a team runs this before paying for a survey

Fieldwork costs thousands of dollars and takes weeks, and by then a bad question is already in the field. A simulated panel shows you what you would get first.

How this is measured, and all 11 checks

Every prediction is scored against the real survey answer. On accuracy Drex now finishes ahead of Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and level with a plain national average, and both are printed next to its own. That is because the group answer is blended with the whole-country split: 90% country, 10% Drex. Without the blend the raw group prediction scores 0.223, well behind both.

Predict a group

Pick a question and a group. Drex answers with a share for every option, and the real survey answer appears beside it.

The real answers come from the General Social Survey, which has interviewed a few thousand Americans a year since 1972 and publishes what they said. The 2024 round covered 3,986 people by interview, web and phone, and we score against those answers.

Scoreboard

Every pair we checked, scored once. Error is the share of answers sitting on the wrong option: 0 is perfect, 0.27 means 27% misplaced. The whole-country guess column uses the same survey question answered by all 3,986 respondents, weighted, then assumes every group answers that way — the score to beat.

Panel run

Ask the same question as 100 different real respondents, one call each, with each person's recorded demographics as the only input, then add up the answers. It shows how fast Drex works through a panel.

Wording test

A prediction is useful when it survives a change of words. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite writes four other ways to ask the same question. Drex checks that each one means the same thing (score 0.90 or higher), then we recompute the answer for each wording. If the answer moves, the prediction is fragile.

Real answers: General Social Survey 2024, NORC at the University of Chicago · weighted so the sample matches the national population.
