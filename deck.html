Can a decision model predict what Americans answer?

A questionnaire PDF goes in. Drex predicts the country's answer. The real General Social Survey is the referee — 3,986 respondents, 2024, official weights.

Live: site-9744494842a94abab91693c343f61169.freebuff.page · NDI + Drex + Gemini recorded baseline

Why this is a fair fight

  • The referee is real survey data, not a rubric we wrote: GSS 2024, weighted by wtssnrps_as.
  • Every prediction is scored against the actual weighted split for that group.
  • Two baselines always on screen: the national average and uniform.
  • The frontier model is recorded once, sequentially, and labelled recorded — never a live retry until it looks good.

How it works

PDF→NDI parse + citations→Drex: same question? (0.80) →persona + options→Drex probabilities→real GSS split

What we would do next

  • Move to per-person prediction: given a respondent's demographics and their earlier answers, predict their next answer — individual ground truth, no averaging to hide in.
  • Ask Drex calibration questions (noul at fixed thresholds) instead of reading a choice distribution as shares.
  • Expand beyond eleven pairs once the unverified items get proper GSS counterparts.
